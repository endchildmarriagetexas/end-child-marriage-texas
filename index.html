Every Child Deserves a Childhood

We are a coalition of survivors, advocates, parents, faith leaders, educators, and community members working to end child marriage in Texas and protect the rights and futures of all children.

About Us

The Texas Coalition to End Child Marriage advocates for policies, education, and survivor support initiatives that protect children from being forced or pressured into marriage before adulthood.


Our Mission

To educate Texans, support survivors, and advance laws that protect children from child marriage.


Our Vision

A Texas where every child has the opportunity to complete their education, pursue their dreams, and decide their future free from coercion.

Education

Child Marriage and Education

Education opens doors to opportunity, independence, and personal growth. Child marriage often closes those doors too soon.

Children who marry are significantly more likely to leave school before completing their education. Many struggle to balance school responsibilities with the demands of marriage, pregnancy, parenting, or financial pressures.

Research has shown that children who marry before age 18 are less likely to graduate from high school and are less likely to attend or complete college. As a result, child marriage can limit career opportunities, earning potential, and financial independence later in life.

When children remain in school, they are more likely to develop critical skills, pursue higher education, secure stable employment, and make informed decisions about their futures.

A child belongs in a classroom, not a marriage.

Every child deserves the opportunity to complete their education, pursue their dreams, and choose their own path before entering marriage.

Child marriage can interrupt schooling and limit future educational opportunities.

Economic Impact

Economic Impact of Child Marriage

Child marriage can have lifelong economic consequences for children, families, and communities.

Children who marry are more likely to leave school early, reducing their opportunities to gain education, job skills, and professional experience. Without a high school diploma or higher education, many face limited employment opportunities and lower lifetime earnings.

Early marriage is often associated with increased financial hardship. Children who marry may become financially dependent on a spouse, face barriers to stable employment, and experience a higher risk of poverty throughout adulthood.

Communities are also affected. When children are unable to complete their education and reach their full potential, local economies lose valuable talent, productivity, and leadership.

Investing in education and delaying marriage until adulthood helps children achieve greater economic independence, strengthens families, and contributes to healthier and more prosperous communities.

Every child deserves the opportunity to build a future before taking on the responsibilities of marriage.

Early marriage can contribute to long-term financial hardship and reduced earning potential.

Health & Safety

Health & Safety

Child marriage can have serious effects on a child's physical health, emotional well-being, safety, and long-term quality of life.

Children who marry often face increased risks of domestic violence, coercion, social isolation, and mental health challenges. Many are placed in adult situations before they are emotionally, physically, or financially prepared.

Child marriage has also been associated with higher rates of anxiety, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and other trauma-related conditions. The loss of childhood, combined with the pressures of marriage and parenting, can have lasting effects on mental and emotional health.

Early marriage can limit access to education, supportive peer relationships, and important developmental opportunities that help children build independence and confidence.

Children in marriages may experience increased stress and isolation while taking on responsibilities that are typically associated with adulthood.

Every child deserves to grow up in a safe environment, free to learn, develop healthy relationships, and make major life decisions as an adult.

Children deserve safety, education, opportunity, mental well-being, and the freedom to choose their own future.

Childern in early marriages may face increased risks related to mental health, domestic violence, and isolation.

Human Rights

Human Rights and Child Marriage

Child marriage is globally recognized as a human rights issue because it can deny children the ability to make informed decisions about their own lives and futures.

Every child has the right to safety, education, health, and the opportunity to reach their full potential. When a child enters a marriage, those rights may be limited or interrupted by adult responsibilities that they are not prepared to assume.

Child marriage can affect a child's access to education, economic opportunities, healthcare, and personal freedom. It may also increase vulnerability to coercion, abuse, social isolation, anxiety, depression, PTSD, and other long-term mental health challenges.

Children deserve the opportunity to grow, learn, develop healthy relationships, and make major life decisions as adults. Protecting children from marriage before adulthood helps safeguard their rights and supports healthier futures for individuals, families, and communities.

The fight to end child marriage is not only about changing laws. It is about ensuring that every child is treated with dignity, respect, and the freedom to choose their own path in life.

Every child deserves a childhood, an education, and the freedom to choose their future.

Children deserve the right to grow, learn, and make life-altering decisions as adults.

Tammi's Story

I was married in Lampasas, Texas, in May 2005, just one month after my 15th birthday.

Like many survivors of child marriage, I entered a life-changing legal and personal commitment before I had the opportunity to fully understand its consequences. While childhood should have been a time for education, personal growth, and planning for the future, marriage dramatically altered the course of my life.

For years, I carried the effects of that experience. Child marriage impacted my opportunities, my voice, and my ability to make decisions about my own future. As I learned more about child marriage laws in the United States, I was shocked to discover that I was not alone.

Since 2000, more than 41,000 minors have been married in Texas, and that number continues to grow every year. Behind every number is a child whose future may be altered by a decision made before reaching adulthood.

Rather than remaining silent, I chose to become an advocate.

Today, I work to raise awareness about the realities of child marriage, support survivors, educate communities, and advocate for stronger protections for children. I believe every child deserves the chance to grow up, complete their education, pursue their dreams, and make their own choices about marriage and adulthood.

This coalition was created to bring together survivors, advocates, parents, educators, faith leaders, and community members who believe that childhood should never be cut short by marriage.

Together, we can create a future where every child is protected, empowered, and free to choose their own path.

Featured Book

Have You Ever Worn a Scarlet Letter: Child Marriage in America, by Tammi Wooten, a Texas child marriage survivor, exposes one of the most overlooked human rights failures in the United States — the ongoing legality and devastating impact of child marriage. Through investigative research, survivor accounts, legal analysis, and historical context, this book reveals how loopholes in American law continue to allow minors to be married, often to adults, with lifelong consequences.
